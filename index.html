How it works

Every route begins with someone who knows the way.

Directionary brings together route knowledge shared by its community, including people with visual impairments, making directions, clues and landmarks accessible to others.

Through this shared knowledge, people can discover unfamiliar journeys, learn routes and contribute their own experiences.

Learning a route

Review a route’s directions, clues and landmarks before you travel, and return to them to reinforce what you have learned.

Exploring and contributing

Explore routes and add what you know. Your experience of a journey can help someone else become familiar with it.

Members of the community are not only following directions. They are where the directions come from.

Why it exists

Three challenges, one shared answer.

  1. 01

    Learning and remembering routes

    A route can involve many steps and details. Keeping them in one place makes them easier to learn and to revisit.

  2. 02

    Exploring unfamiliar places independently

    New places are harder to approach without familiarity. Routes shared by others help build it beforehand.

  3. 03

    Finding route information that fits

    Detailed route information described for people with visual impairments is not always available. Directionary brings community knowledge together to help.

The framework

Directions, clues and landmarks.

Routes are described using an O&M-aligned framework. Each part answers a different question a traveller might have.

01

Directions

Where do I move?

Instructions describing where to move.

02

Clues

How do I recognise where I am?

Environmental and sensory information that helps someone recognise their surroundings.

03

Landmarks

What can I orient myself by?

Recognisable reference points that help someone orient themselves.

The framework supports route learning. It does not replace orientation and mobility training.

Philosophy

Designed with purpose.

Three principles shape how we approach accessibility, clarity and independence.

  1. 01

    Ear First, Eyes Second

    We prioritise experiences that can be understood beyond what is seen.

    By designing for screen readers first, we aim to make information and interactions clear, meaningful and accessible.

  2. 02

    Less but Clear

    Simplicity should never come at the expense of clarity.

    We remove unnecessary complexity while ensuring people have the information they need to understand each interaction.

  3. 03

    Empower, Not Replace

    Directionary complements orientation and mobility training rather than replacing it.

    Our goal is to make shared route knowledge more accessible, encouraging exploration, familiarity and independence.

About

Knowledge shared. Confidence built.

Learning a new route often involves understanding directions, recognising landmarks and remembering the details that make a journey familiar.

Directionary was created to make this knowledge easier to access and share.

By bringing community-contributed route information together, we hope to support people with visual impairments as they explore and familiarise themselves with new environments. We believe technology should complement existing skills, not replace them.

A different approach to wayfinding

Directionary began as a design initiative exploring how route knowledge could be made more accessible to people with visual impairments. The project focuses on supporting route learning through community-contributed directions, clues and landmarks.

Directionary is developed in collaboration with Guide Dogs Singapore.

Responsible wayfinding

A guide, not a replacement.

Directionary is a supplementary route-learning and wayfinding tool.

Routes may contain community-contributed information that becomes outdated or does not reflect current environmental conditions. Directionary does not provide real-time obstacle detection or guarantee route accuracy or safety.

Continue to exercise personal judgement, stay aware of your surroundings and use appropriate mobility skills and aids.
