Find your way, your way.

Directionary is a community-driven route-learning and wayfinding app for people with visual impairments. Create, share and learn routes through directions, clues and landmarks.

Developed in collaboration with Guide Dogs Singapore.

Why it exists

Learning a new route is hard to do alone.

Directionary makes route knowledge easier to record, revisit and share.

People who are visually impaired often face three challenges:

  • learning and remembering routes;
  • exploring unfamiliar places independently; and
  • finding detailed route information that is relevant to them.

Directionary brings community knowledge together to help with all three.

What makes it different

Every route begins with someone who knows the way.

Directionary’s value comes from community-contributed knowledge, including the lived experiences of people with visual impairments.

Through this shared knowledge, people can discover unfamiliar journeys, learn routes and contribute their own experiences. Members of the community are not only following directions. They are where the directions come from.

How it works

Create. Share. Learn.

Directionary supports both people learning a route and experienced travellers who explore and contribute.

  1. 01

    Create

    Record a route you know, with the directions, clues and landmarks that make it familiar.

  2. 02

    Share

    Make it available to the community so that others can find it.

  3. 03

    Learn

    Review a route before you travel, and return to it to reinforce what you have learned.

The framework

Directions, clues and landmarks.

Routes are described using an O&M-aligned framework. Each part answers a different question a traveller might have.

  • Directions

    Where do I move?

    Instructions describing where to move.

  • Clues

    How do I recognise where I am?

    Environmental and sensory information that helps someone recognise their surroundings.

  • Landmarks

    What can I orient myself by?

    Recognisable reference points that help someone orient themselves.

The framework supports route learning. It does not replace orientation and mobility training.

Philosophy

Designed with purpose.

Three principles shape how we approach accessibility, clarity and independence.

  1. 01

    Ear First, Eyes Second

    We prioritise experiences that can be understood beyond what is seen. By designing for screen readers first, we aim to make information and interactions clear, meaningful and accessible.

  2. 02

    Less but Clear

    Simplicity should never come at the expense of clarity. We remove unnecessary complexity while ensuring people have the information they need to understand each interaction.

  3. 03

    Empower, Not Replace

    Directionary complements orientation and mobility training rather than replacing it. Our goal is to make shared route knowledge more accessible, encouraging exploration, familiarity and independence.

Responsible wayfinding

A guide, not a replacement.

Directionary is a supplementary route-learning and wayfinding tool that complements orientation and mobility training and existing mobility skills.

Routes may contain community-contributed information that becomes outdated or does not reflect current environmental conditions. Directionary does not provide real-time obstacle detection or guarantee route accuracy or safety.

Continue to exercise personal judgement, stay aware of your surroundings and use appropriate mobility skills and aids.

About

Knowledge shared. Confidence built.

Learning a new route often involves understanding directions, recognising landmarks and remembering the details that make a journey familiar. Directionary was created to make this knowledge easier to access and share.

Directionary began as a design initiative exploring how route knowledge could be made more accessible to people with visual impairments. It was developed through research, testing and collaboration with visually impaired users and orientation and mobility professionals. We will share verified findings here when they are ready.
